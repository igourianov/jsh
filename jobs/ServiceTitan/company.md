# ServiceTitan

- **Company Type:** Vertical SaaS / Field Service Management Platform (the trades)
- **Stage:** Public (NASDAQ: TTAN, IPO Dec 2024)
- **Size:** ~3,000+ employees (offices in Glendale/LA, Atlanta, Yerevan Armenia)
- **Remote Policy:** Hybrid with some fully remote roles. R&D reports good WLB and remote flexibility; US (LA, Atlanta), Canada remote and Yerevan engineering hub

## Quick Take
- Profitable-trajectory public vertical SaaS leader. ~$961M FY2026 revenue and Q1 FY2027 (ended Apr 2026) revenue of $268.8M growing 25% YoY, on track for ~$1.13B FY2027. Financially solid, not a startup gamble.
- Strong .NET/C# engineering shop operating at real scale (100M+ jobs, 45M customers, 50M locations). Good fit for a manager with deep .NET background.
- Key concern: Glassdoor engineering ratings are mediocre (Senior SWE ~3.1, overall 3.4). Recurring complaints about weak lower-level eng management, siloed teams, limited promotion/growth and talent attrition. Experience varies heavily by team.

## Milestones
- 2007-2012: Founded by Ara Mahdessian and Vahe Kuzoyan (Armenian-American immigrants, USC software engineering grads); cloud platform launched 2012
- 2015: Series A funding
- 2018: Crossed $100M ARR
- 2019: Opened Yerevan, Armenia engineering office (now a major eng hub)
- 2021: Surpassed $250M ARR
- 2022-2023: Launched Titan Intelligence (TI), purpose-built AI suite for the trades; Feb 2023 layoff of ~8% of workforce (~221 employees)
- Dec 2024: IPO on NASDAQ (TTAN), raised ~$625M (some sources cite ~$1.4B total proceeds)
- 2025-2026: Atlas AI assistant + AI Voice Agents rollout; FY2026 (ended Jan 2026) $961M revenue, ~10,800 active customers
- Q1 FY2027 (reported Jun 2026): $268.8M revenue (+25% YoY), NDR >110%, GAAP operating loss narrowed to $25.8M, non-GAAP operating income $40.8M (15.2% margin); full-year FY2027 guidance $1.13B-$1.14B

## Company & Product
ServiceTitan is the operating system for the trades. Its all-in-one cloud platform automates scheduling, dispatching, invoicing, payments, marketing, reporting, accounting, payroll and financing for home and commercial service businesses (HVAC, plumbing, electrical, lawn care, pest control etc). It is the dominant vertical SaaS player in this space. Business model is subscription plus usage revenue (payments, financing, marketing). FY2026: 21% Q4 revenue growth, NDR >110%, gross dollar retention >95%, path to 25% operating margins. Founders still lead the company.

## Engineering Culture
Engineering organized into specialized teams (16+ noted in their blog) covering infrastructure, data/ML, mobile, dispatch optimization, CRM, accounting/payroll and reporting. They maintain a Medium engineering blog ("Titan Tech") emphasizing knowledge sharing, high code quality, strict coding standards and continuous refactoring. Heavy investment in AI/ML: Titan Intelligence, Smart/Smarter Dispatch routing, Atlas natural-language assistant and AI Voice Agents. Real technical challenges around 24/7 availability, disaster recovery, offline mobile sync and big-data scale. Downside per reviews: teams are siloed by specialty with limited cross-team collaboration, and lower-level engineering management quality is inconsistent.

## Tech Stack
- **Backend:** C# / .NET and .NET Core, ASP.NET MVC (primary server stack)
- **Frontend:** React, TypeScript (modern UI), legacy JavaScript
- **Mobile:** React, historically Apache Cordova
- **ML / Data:** Python, RedShift, ElasticSearch
- **Databases:** Microsoft SQL Server (primary), Redis (cache)
- **Cloud:** Azure (primary), AWS (backup cluster)
- **Scale:** 100M+ jobs, 45M customers, 50M locations, millions of user actions per hour

## Team Health
- Glassdoor overall ~3.4/5 (800+ reviews); Software Engineer/Senior SWE ratings ~3.1/5
- Compensation and benefits rated well (~4.0/5)
- Indeed: ~3.7/5 WLB, ~3.8/5 culture
- Positives: clear and motivating mission, real customer impact, sustainable pace and autonomy on many teams, strong comp
- Negatives: limited growth/promotion, siloed teams, inconsistent eng management, reports of good engineers leaving
- Strong recurring theme: quality of experience depends heavily on your specific team and manager

## Business Stability
Strong. Public, growing revenue 25% YoY in Q1 FY2027 ($268.8M; ~$961M for FY2026), heading toward ~$1.13B-$1.14B FY2027 on company guidance. Improving margins (non-GAAP operating margin 15.2% in Q1 FY2027, GAAP operating loss narrowing to -9.6%), strong free cash flow, NDR >110%, gross transaction volume $21.7B (+23%). Market leader in a large underpenetrated vertical. Only prior workforce reduction was an 8% cut (~221 employees) in February 2023; no recorded company-wide layoff events since on major layoff trackers as of mid-2026 (only scattered community forum chatter). Low business-failure risk relative to typical scale-ups.

## Red Flags
- Mediocre engineering Glassdoor ratings (~3.1 SWE) with complaints about weak lower-level engineering management lacking clear strategy and vision
- Siloed teams with little cross-team collaboration
- Limited promotion and career growth; reports of senior talent attrition
- Some Blind reviews cite "toxic, incompetent execs/leadership" and exec visibility only in all-hands or crises (note: Blind reviews skew negative and are unverified)
- WLB is team-dependent; some report constant priority churn driven by execs
- For an Engineering Manager: the recurring "depends on your team / weak EM layer" theme is the main thing to probe. It could be an opportunity (room to bring strong leadership) or a structural problem
