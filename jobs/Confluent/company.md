# Confluent

**Company Type:** SaaS Platform (Data Streaming Infrastructure)
**Stage:** Public (NASDAQ: CFLT, IBM Acquisition Pending)
**Size:** 3,060 employees (as of December 2024)
**Remote Policy:** Remote-first, globally distributed across 18+ locations

## Quick Take
- Founded by original Apache Kafka creators, dominant contributor (75%+) to Kafka open source
- IBM acquisition announced Dec 2025 ($11B), closing mid-2026 - creates significant uncertainty around culture and direction
- Glassdoor ratings show concerning trends: 3.4/5 overall, only 46% would recommend (down 50% in past year), 2.9/5 work-life balance
- Talented engineering teams working on complex distributed systems at massive scale, but operational burden and execution challenges mentioned in reviews
- Major red flag: Cultural fit concerns with IBM integration, potential talent exodus post-acquisition

## Company & Product

Confluent develops a data streaming platform built around Apache Kafka, enabling real-time information flow across organizations. Founded September 2014 by Jay Kreps, Jun Rao, and Neha Narkhede (original Kafka creators at LinkedIn), the company provides Confluent Cloud (fully managed Kafka service) and Confluent Platform (self-managed) with additional tools like Schema Registry, ksqlDB, Cluster Linking, and 100+ pre-built connectors.

The Kora Global team (this role's focus) manages multi-region replication capabilities including Cluster Linking and Multi Region Clusters, operating thousands of clusters across AWS, GCP, and Azure for enterprise customers requiring disaster recovery, global data sharing, and real-time analytics at scale.

IBM announced acquisition on December 8, 2025, valuing Confluent at $11 billion, with expected close by mid-2026. IBM's stated goal is creating a "smart data platform for enterprise generative AI" by combining Confluent's streaming capabilities with IBM's watsonx AI platform.

## Engineering Culture

Strong technical focus with opportunities to work on complex distributed systems at massive scale. Confluent engineers contribute over 75% of Apache Kafka open source commits, demonstrating deep investment in both open source and commercial innovation. The company has re-architected its streaming engine with millions of engineering hours for cloud-native capabilities.

Engineering blog at confluent.io/blog provides technical tutorials, best practices, and industry insights. Company emphasizes collaborative leadership culture without egos, welcoming diverse perspectives across time zones.

However, recent reviews indicate execution challenges: "Innovation doesn't go further than striving to fall short of Amazon without company operations blowing up. Learning opportunities are limited by brain-dead operations work." Cultural concerns exist around IBM integration - employees accustomed to agile, open-source-driven culture may struggle with IBM's more traditional corporate environment.

## Tech Stack

**Core:** Apache Kafka, Java, Scala (Kafka ecosystem)
**Cloud:** AWS, GCP, Azure (multi-cloud strategy)
**Frontend:** React, jQuery
**Infrastructure:** NGINX
**Platform Tools:** Schema Registry, ksqlDB, Cluster Linking, REST Proxy, Confluent Platform for Apache Flink

Primarily Java/JVM-based distributed systems architecture with cloud-native deployment models across all major public clouds.

## Business Stability

**Funding:** Public company (NASDAQ: CFLT), IBM acquisition pending at $11B valuation

**Recent Events:**
- December 2025: IBM acquisition announced, deal expected to close mid-2026
- January 2023: Laid off 221 employees (8% of workforce) citing challenging economic environment, longer sales cycles, increased customer scrutiny
- 2024: Workforce grew 11.52% to 3,060 employees

**Revenue Model:** SaaS subscriptions for Confluent Cloud (managed service) and Confluent Platform licenses (self-managed), plus professional services and support.

IBM acquisition creates both opportunity (enterprise AI focus, IBM's enterprise relationships) and risk (integration challenges, cultural misalignment, talent retention). Transaction expected to be cash-funded from IBM's balance sheet.

## Red Flags

**CRITICAL - IBM Acquisition Uncertainty:**
- Deal announced Dec 2025, closing mid-2026 creates 6+ months of limbo
- Cultural alignment concerns: agile, open-source culture vs IBM's traditional corporate environment
- Industry analysis warns: "employees used to agility often struggle with stodgy corporate culture, and once retention incentives expire, talent exodus is common"
- IBM recently laid off ~8,000 employees (mostly HR), raising questions about post-acquisition staffing strategy
- CEO message says "roles, managers, pay, benefits stay the same until close" - no guarantees after

**Declining Employee Sentiment:**
- Glassdoor recommendation rate dropped 50% in past year (now 46%)
- Work-life balance rated 2.9/5
- Reviews mention "layoffs come out of nowhere" and lack of transparency

**Operational Challenges:**
- Reviews cite "brain-dead operations work" limiting engineering growth
- Execution and management issues mentioned
- "Innovation doesn't go further than striving to fall short of Amazon"

**Timing Consideration:**
- Joining during acquisition = maximum uncertainty
- Role responsibilities may shift post-close
- Reporting structure, strategy, roadmap all subject to change
- Retention packages likely prioritize pre-acquisition employees over new hires
