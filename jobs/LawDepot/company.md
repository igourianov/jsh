# LawDepot

- **Company Type:** Product / B2C SaaS (DIY legal documents)
- **Stage:** Enterprise (mature, private, bootstrapped)
- **Size:** ~193 employees (March 2026, third-party estimate); posting says 201-500
- **Remote Policy:** Remote across Canada or hybrid from Edmonton, AB (per posting)

## Quick Take
- Profitable-looking, unfunded, founder-era private company. Division of Sequiter Software Inc. Low volatility, low visibility.
- Glassdoor rating around 4.1 on ~100 reviews. Engineers praise culture and management support, and complain about legacy code.
- Actively investing in AI (hiring a Product Owner of AI Projects and a manager to lead AI adoption). Software Developer median pay is reported around 72k CAD, which suggests a conservative pay culture. Verify the manager band with the recruiter.

## Milestones
- 2001: Founder Ken Sawyer needs a promissory note, finds hiring a lawyer inconvenient and starts building document-generation software
- 2002: LAWDEPOT trademark in use in Canada
- Later: seven-time Growth 500 ranked, Top Employer recognition, claims over ten million customers and over two million monthly site visitors
- No acquisitions, funding rounds, IPO or layoffs found in the past 5 years

## Company & Product
LawDepot publishes do-it-yourself legal documents built around a step-by-step guided questionnaire. Categories include estate planning, family law, real estate, business and HR. Sold in multiple jurisdictions (CA, US and others) via a consumer website. Headquartered in Edmonton (9644 54 Avenue NW), operated as a division of Sequiter Software Inc. Employees are reported across North America and Europe.

## Engineering Culture
- No public engineering blog, open source organization or conference presence found.
- Reviews mention strong management support for career growth, helpful coworkers and technology that is kept up to date.
- Posting describes Agile teams with Scrum Masters, Product Owners, internal CMS editor platform and website experimentation with Product and Analytics.

## Tech Stack
C#/.NET, Entity Framework Core, SQL Server, TypeScript/JavaScript, Vue (from the posting). Internal CMS editor used to author document templates. Some applications are legacy code.

## Team Health
- Glassdoor: about 4.1 of 5 on roughly 101 reviews. Compensation and benefits about 3.5.
- Pros: culture, helpful coworkers, job security, management support, engagement activities.
- Cons: legacy code that is hard to work with, uneven code quality, below-market pay for developers.
- Glassdoor pages could not be fetched directly (403). Figures come from search summaries.

## Business Stability
Bootstrapped and unfunded with a 20+ year history and a durable consumer brand. Risks are dependence on SEO and search traffic, and disruption of DIY legal templates by general-purpose AI assistants. The AI hiring suggests leadership is responding to that.

## Red Flags
- Legacy code and inconsistent quality reported by developers.
- Below-market developer pay reported, which may cap the manager band.
- Very little public information on engineering practices or leadership.
- Existential product risk from generative AI substituting for template-based legal documents.
