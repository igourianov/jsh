# CaptivateIQ

- **Company Type:** SaaS
- **Stage:** Late-stage venture-backed (unicorn, Series C)
- **Size:** ~260-280 employees (down from ~300+ in prior years)
- **Remote Policy:** Fully remote-first (some roles hybrid, e.g. Austin office); team spread across 30+ states, 10+ countries

## Quick Take
- No-code incentive compensation management (ICM) platform for mid-market/enterprise sales orgs, competing with Xactly, Varicent, Spiff.
- Product and leadership consistently praised; but recruiting/company narrative ("transparent, friendly") is undercut by repeated, unannounced layoffs per Glassdoor.
- Funding has been flat since the 2022 Series C ($100M, $1.25B valuation) - no new raise reported through mid-2026, worth probing runway/profitability in interviews.

## Milestones
- Founded 2017, San Francisco.
- Reached unicorn status in 2022 (5 years after founding) with $100M Series C at $1.25B valuation.
- November 2022: first reported layoffs.
- 2025: platform relaunch combining incentive compensation with sales planning (August 2025); Guided Plan Builder launch (June 2025); named a Leader in The Forrester Wave for Sales Performance Management, Q1 2025.
- May 2026: launched a portfolio of AI agents automating steps across the compensation/sales-planning lifecycle.

## Company & Product
CaptivateIQ builds a no-code commission/incentive compensation management platform: a real-time calculation engine plus a batch processing engine for cyclical calculations, backed by an automated, scalable data sync and storage layer. Recent product direction has expanded from pure ICM into sales planning and, most recently, agentic AI features for compensation workflows. Co-founded and still led by co-CEOs Mark Schopmeyer and Conway Teng, with co-founder Hubert Wong as CTO - a stable founding leadership team, unusual for a company at this stage.

## Engineering Culture
Public engineering content is thin - a hackathon recap and an architecture overview post are essentially the only engineering blog material found (captivateiq.com/blog). No visible open-source presence. Glassdoor engineering-specific reviews describe the environment as surprisingly non-chaotic for a startup, with "fun scalability problems," engaged product partners, and transparent leadership. Interview loop for engineering roles: HR screen, manager interview, coding/system design round(s), culture-fit/leadership round - roughly a month end to end.

## Tech Stack
Limited public detail. Known: Tauri (desktop bundler) integrated into the existing stack; a real-time calculation engine and separate batch processing engine; a dedicated data sync/storage layer designed for scale. Security/compliance (auth, authorization, audit logging) described as built through the full stack. No further language/framework/cloud specifics surfaced in public sources - worth asking directly in interviews.

## Team Health
- Glassdoor: 3.9/5 overall (141 reviews), 3.7 work-life balance, 3.7 culture/values, 3.6 career opportunities, 65% would recommend to a friend.
- Positive themes: strong hiring bar, competent peers, leadership seen as genuinely invested in employee success.
- Negative themes: manager quality inconsistent for some (client-facing experience gaps cited), bonus-dependent compensation with opaque/adjustable criteria, "grind" feeling with limited team chemistry-building, one review titled "Used to be great, going downhill as a place to work."

## Business Stability
Raised $168M total; last round was the $100M Series C (Jan 2022) at $1.25B valuation - no newer funding round reported as of mid-2026, over four years since last raise. Headcount has drifted down (~300 -> ~260-280). Product releases have continued at a steady pace through 2025-2026 (platform relaunch, AI agents), suggesting continued investment despite headcount pressure. No IPO or acquisition activity found.

## Red Flags
- Multiple, recurring rounds of layoffs since Nov 2022, several described by employees as unannounced/unacknowledged even to affected teams - notable dissonance with the company's public "transparent" self-image.
- No funding round in 4+ years despite unicorn status; combined with headcount decline, raises questions about runway, growth trajectory, or a deliberate move toward profitability (worth asking directly).
- Compensation weighted toward variable bonuses with employee-reported opacity in bonus criteria - ironic for a company that sells compensation-transparency software.
- Very sparse public engineering output (blog, OSS) makes it hard to independently verify engineering practices/tech stack beyond what's asked in interviews.
