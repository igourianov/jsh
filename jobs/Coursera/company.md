# Company Research: Coursera

**Company Type:** B2C/B2B EdTech SaaS Platform \
**Stage:** Public (NASDAQ: COUR) \
**Remote Policy:** Remote-first with optional hubs and offices

## Quick Take

- Publicly traded EdTech leader with 190M+ learners, strong GenAI catalog growth (6x expansion in 2024), and first profitable year in 2024
- Engineering team of ~378 (26% of workforce) with solid Glassdoor rating (4.2/5 for engineers) and modern tech stack (Java/Scala/Python, React/TypeScript, AWS/GCP)
- Two significant layoffs (2022 and late 2024 - 10% workforce cut) due to slowing enterprise growth and declining subscriber retention, with new CEO transition in February 2025

## Company & Product

Coursera was founded in 2012 by Stanford professors Andrew Ng and Daphne Koller to make world-class learning accessible globally. Today serves over 190 million learners and partners with 375+ universities and industry leaders to provide online learning in AI, data science, technology, and business. Headquartered in Mountain View, California, Coursera operates as a Delaware public benefit corporation and Certified B Corp.

As of December 31, 2024, Coursera employed 1,260 people (down 35 employees or -2.70% from prior year). The company maintains 4 main office locations and 32 global hubs, operating as a remote-first organization with flexible work arrangements.

The Skills and Credentials team (relevant to the Engineering Manager role) builds foundational infrastructure connecting Coursera's content to career outcomes, including core skills taxonomy, mapping services, skill progression infrastructure, and scalable architecture for millions of learners.

## Engineering Culture

Coursera maintains an active engineering presence with:

**Open Source:** Active GitHub organization ([coursera.github.io](https://coursera.github.io/)) contributing projects and building on established technologies like Play, Android, Nginx, Ubuntu, and React. The company emphasizes "using and contributing to open source at Coursera" as organizational philosophy.

**Engineering Resources:**
- Engineering Blog on Medium (@CourseraEng)
- Tech Talks video content
- App Platform documentation for developers
- Active social media presence

**Team Structure:** Engineering represents the largest department with 378 employees (26% of workforce). Other teams include Marketing and Product (300, 21%), Sales and Support (198, 14%), Finance and Administration (138, 9%), and Business Management (112, 8%).

## Tech Stack & Architecture

**Backend:** Java, Scala, Python \
**Frontend:** React, TypeScript \
**Databases:** Relational and NoSQL datastores \
**Cloud:** AWS, GCP \
**Infrastructure:** Kubernetes (inferred from job postings) \
**APIs:** GraphQL, gRPC (mentioned as desired skills)

Job postings indicate Coursera seeks engineers with experience in distributed systems, microservices architecture, and full-stack development. The platform serves millions of concurrent learners, indicating significant scale challenges.

## Product & Market Position

**Market Leadership:** Coursera is a dominant player in online education, partnering with top-tier universities (Stanford, Yale, University of Michigan) and tech companies (Google, Meta, Microsoft, Amazon, NVIDIA).

**GenAI Expansion:** For the second consecutive year, GenAI is the fastest-growing skill on Coursera. Partners published over 450 GenAI courses in 2024 (expanding catalog by 6x), averaging about one new GenAI course daily. Since ChatGPT's launch, these courses have amassed over 4 million enrollments. Job postings mentioning GenAI have grown more than 4x in 2024.

**Business Model:** Dual revenue streams from individual subscriptions (Consumer segment) and enterprise partnerships (Enterprise segment). Consumer segment showed 13% year-over-year growth in Q3 2025, while Enterprise segment faces challenges.

## Engineering Team Health

**Glassdoor Ratings:**
- Overall company: 3.6/5 (based on 646+ reviews)
- Software Engineers: 4.2/5 (based on 42 reviews)
- 61% would recommend to a friend

**Common Pros (from 2024 engineering reviews):**
- Reasonable work-life balance compared to rest of Bay Area
- Friendly coworkers and collaborative culture
- Unlimited PTO with company-wide rest days
- Smart people and great technology
- Good managers and skip-level leadership (generally)

**Common Cons (from 2024 engineering reviews):**
- Management is bad and disorganized in some areas; promotions can take a long time, especially with bad manager who won't advocate
- No clear direction with leadership; constant layoffs and reorgs
- Growth opportunities for senior folks can be difficult depending on team
- Company culture declining overall, less motivation and passion
- Limited full-stack opportunities (roles tend to be either frontend or backend)

**Work-Life Balance:** Generally rated positively, with flexible remote-first policy and unlimited PTO.

## Business Stability

**Financial Performance (2024-2025):**
- FY 2024 revenue: $694.7M (up 9.3% from FY 2023)
- Q3 2025 revenue: $194M (up 10% year-over-year)
- Full year 2025 revenue outlook: $750-$754M
- First full year of positive Adjusted EBITDA in 2024: $41.5M (6.0% of revenue)
- Generated $59M+ in Free Cash Flow in 2024

**Stock Performance:** Public company (NASDAQ: COUR). Stock dropped ~20% overnight in late October 2024 following Q3 earnings and layoff announcement.

**Layoff History:**
1. **November 2022:** Company-wide layoffs affecting all organizations and regions due to deteriorating macroeconomic environment and need to optimize post-pandemic expansion. Provided 4 months base pay plus additional compensation, healthcare for up to 4 months, job placement assistance.

2. **Late October 2024:** 10% workforce reduction (~150 employees) to align costs with business goals. California WARN notice filed for 67 layoffs at Palo Alto office (including 8 VPs, several directors/managers) with separations planned for December 23, 2024. Expected to save $30M annually. Triggered by declining revenue projections and challenges in enterprise business and subscriber retention.

**Leadership Transition:** Jeff Maggioncalda retired as CEO after 7 years, replaced by Greg Hart as President and CEO effective February 3, 2025.

**Business Challenges:**
- Enterprise business (which thrived during pandemic) is now faltering
- While new course enrollments remain robust, users are not maintaining paid subscriptions as long as previously
- Slowing revenue growth and environmental uncertainty

## Concerns for Engineering Managers

**Recent Instability:**
- Two layoffs within 2 years (2022 and 2024), with 2024 cuts affecting senior leadership (8 VPs laid off)
- CEO transition in February 2025 after 7-year tenure could signal strategic shifts
- Stock performance concerns with ~20% drop after recent earnings

**Organization & Culture:**
- "Constant layoffs and reorgs" mentioned in recent reviews
- "No direction with leadership" cited as con
- "Company culture declining overall, less motivation and passion"
- Management quality varies significantly by team ("will take you forever to get a promotion especially if you have a bad manager")

**Career Growth:**
- "Growth opportunities for senior folks can be difficult depending on team"
- Limited full-stack opportunities (roles siloed as frontend or backend)

**Business Headwinds:**
- Enterprise segment struggling (post-pandemic decline)
- Subscriber retention declining
- Need to balance rapid feature delivery with platform health in uncertain environment
