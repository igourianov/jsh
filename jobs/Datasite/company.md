# Datasite (Grata)

- **Company Type:** SaaS / Product (M&A dealmaking and private-market intelligence platform)
- **Stage:** Private equity-owned, growth-stage (bootstrapped operationally, backed by PE with $500M commitment for expansion)
- **Size:** ~1,450 employees (Datasite parent); Grata itself is a much smaller acquired business unit
- **Remote Policy:** Hybrid flexibility cited generally for Datasite; the Grata Software Engineering Director posting itself is tagged remote (Georgian Bay, Ontario)

## Quick Take
- Grata is a recently acquired (June 2025) strategic business unit inside Datasite, not a standalone company. Culture, stack and stability signals are a mix of Grata-native (AI-native private-market intelligence startup) and Datasite-corporate (60-year-old PE-owned SaaS platform).
- Datasite is on an acquisition spree in 2025 (Grata, Blueflame AI, Sourcescrub, Valu8, Sherpany) funded by controlling shareholder CapVest's $500M commitment. Integration risk and reorg churn are plausible given the pace.
- Glassdoor reviews of Datasite proper flag weak/absent engineering leadership and a CEO seen as disengaged from engineering, despite engineering being core to the business. Worth probing directly whether Grata retains autonomy as a "strategic business unit" or is being folded into Datasite's engineering org and culture.

## Milestones
- 1968: Founded as Merrill Corporation, a financial-printing business.
- 2018: Divested legacy print business, pivoted fully to SaaS M&A platform.
- 2020: Rebranded from Merrill Corporation to Datasite.
- Late 2020: Acquired by CapVest Partners LLP (London-based PE), CapVest's first North American acquisition.
- 2016: Grata founded (New York) as an AI-native private markets intelligence platform, separately from Datasite.
- June 2025: Datasite acquires Grata; CapVest commits $500M (organic + inorganic) to expand Datasite's intelligence solutions. Grata co-founders Andrew Bocskocsky and Nevin Raj continue leading Grata as a strategic business unit.
- August 2025: Datasite acquires Blueflame AI and announces acquisition of Sourcescrub, both expanding private-market intelligence/AI capabilities.
- Datasite also acquired Sherpany (per this job's screening file summary) as part of the same portfolio expansion.

## Company & Product
Datasite is a global SaaS provider of AI-powered workflow, collaboration and automation software for M&A, investment and strategic projects: virtual data rooms, due diligence, outreach, fundraising and restructuring workflows, serving law firms, investment banks, private equity firms and corporate development teams in 170+ countries, with 55,000+ projects captured annually.

Grata, the specific product this job serves, is the private-markets dealmaking data platform: proprietary, searchable data on private companies, their financials and owners, used by PE/growth investors, investment bankers, management consultants and corporate development teams to source and win deals. 700+ customers; recognized as market leader by G2 and PE Wire.

## Engineering Culture
No public engineering blog or GitHub presence found for Datasite or Grata. Glassdoor reviews describe a mixed picture at the Datasite corporate level: some employees cite a fun, tech-forward environment with new technologies being adopted, but multiple reviews describe engineering leadership as weak or "all-but-non-existent," frequent reorganization of product and engineering, and a CEO seen as distant from the engineering division despite it driving revenue. It is unclear from available sources how much of this applies to Grata specifically versus the broader Datasite org, since Grata was only acquired in mid-2025 and is described as operating as a distinct business unit.

## Tech Stack
Public data is thin and low-confidence. Third-party technographic sources (RocketReach) list a broad, generic list including Apache Groovy, Apache JMeter, Apache Maven, ASP.NET, JavaScript, HTML and PHP for Datasite proper — likely reflecting a legacy/enterprise stack rather than Grata's product. The job posting itself calls for Python or Java, AWS, event-driven microservices, ElasticSearch or MongoDB, which is more likely representative of Grata's actual engineering stack (AI-native data platform). No independent confirmation found.

## Team Health
- Datasite overall Glassdoor rating: 3.8/5 (682+ reviews), 69% would recommend to a friend, 53% positive business outlook, compensation/benefits rated 3.8/5.
- Positive: unlimited PTO, solid medical coverage, active adoption of new technologies cited by some reviewers as a "fun" environment.
- Negative: management criticized for undermining engineering culture, frequent reorganization of product/engineering, engineering leadership described as ineffective, and reports of unaddressed issues escalating straight to upper management/CEO.
- No Grata-specific Glassdoor reviews found (recently acquired, likely too new to have a distinct review base).

## Business Stability
- Revenue estimated ~$158-580M depending on source (getlatka estimate $157.9M ARR; ZoomInfo cites $579.8M revenue) — wide variance suggests unreliable third-party estimates rather than disclosed financials.
- PE-owned by CapVest since late 2020; no public signs of financial distress. Active, well-funded acquisition strategy in 2025 (Grata, Blueflame AI, Sourcescrub, Valu8, Sherpany) backed by a $500M CapVest investment commitment suggests the parent company is expanding rather than contracting.
- No layoffs found in searches covering 2025-2026 tech layoff trackers.
- Bootstrapped/no outside VC funding cited by one source (getlatka), consistent with PE ownership rather than venture funding.

## Red Flags
- Reported weak engineering leadership and frequent reorg at Datasite parent per Glassdoor — worth direct questions in interviews about Grata's actual reporting structure and autonomy as a "strategic business unit," and how much Datasite-level dysfunction reaches Grata.
- Rapid-fire 2025 acquisition spree (5+ companies in a matter of months) raises integration risk: overlapping products, culture clashes, and reorg churn are common in this pattern.
- Thin, inconsistent public data on financials and tech stack makes it hard to independently verify claims made in the job posting or by recruiters.
