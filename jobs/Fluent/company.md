# Fluent Inc

- **Company Type:** Performance Marketing / Commerce Media Platform
- **Stage:** Public (NASDAQ: FLNT)
- **Size:** ~250 employees (60 in Product + Tech)
- **Remote Policy:** Hybrid - NYC metro requires Tuesday + Thursday in office; otherwise flexible

## Quick Take
- Publicly traded adtech company undergoing a strategic pivot from legacy performance marketing to Commerce Media Solutions (brand partnerships + post-purchase monetization)
- Commerce Media segment is growing fast (98% YoY through 9 months of 2025) but the overall business is shrinking - total revenue down 22% YTD 2025
- Serious financial risk: "going concern" language in 2024 filings, net losses throughout 2024-2025, and reliance on a new $30M credit facility secured Dec 2025

## Milestones
- **2010** - Founded by Ryan Schulke as a private performance marketing firm in NYC
- **2015** - Acquired by IDI Inc. for $100M, goes public via merger; parent later rebrands to Cogint, Inc.
- **2018** - Cogint spins off data intelligence arm as Red Violet (RDVT), renames itself Fluent, Inc. (FLNT)
- **2019** - Acquires AdParlor (social media advertising platform) for $10M
- **2021** - Don Patrick (former Merkle COO) appointed CEO; company shifts strategy toward higher-margin Commerce Media
- **2023** - Commerce Media Solutions segment launched, achieves triple-digit CAGR from Q1 2023
- **2024** - Discontinues ACA (Affordable Care Act) business; restructuring costs of $1.8M; going concern doubt raised in SEC filings
- **2025** - Sells Winopoly (Call Solutions subsidiary) to InsureCo; secures $30M credit facility; named Ad Age Best Places to Work for 3rd consecutive year
- **Feb 2026** - Q4 2024 results show full-year revenue of $254.6M (down 14.7%); Commerce Media run rate exceeds $85M

## Company & Product
Fluent connects consumer brands with high-intent audiences across the customer journey - from acquisition through post-purchase. The business operates two main segments:

**Commerce Media Solutions** (growth segment): Owned-and-operated media network, brand partnerships (e.g., Authentic Brands), and post-purchase monetization tools. 40% of revenue as of Q3 2025, growing ~98% YoY.

**Owned and Operated / Legacy Segments**: Traditional lead generation and performance marketing campaigns (email, SMS, display). Deliberately being wound down - declined 52% YoY in Q3 2025.

**AdParlor**: Social media advertising platform (Meta, TikTok, etc.) acquired in 2019. Now a smaller revenue contributor (~$2M/quarter).

**Muse AI**: Proprietary AI intelligence platform used for audience targeting and monetization optimization.

## Business Stability
- **Revenue trajectory:** Declining overall (-14.7% in 2024, -22% YTD 2025) as legacy segments are wound down intentionally
- **Commerce Media:** Growing fast but not yet large enough to offset legacy decline; needs to reach ~$150M+ to stabilize total revenue
- **Losses:** Net loss of $29.3M in 2024; $23M in first 9 months of 2025; adjusted EBITDA still negative
- **Cash position:** $9.4M at end of 2024; secured $30M credit facility Dec 2025
- **Going concern:** Explicitly flagged in 2024 SEC filings; as of Q3 2025, company expects Q4 2025 EBITDA profitability and 2026 double-digit revenue growth - but these are projections
- **Market cap:** ~$48-50M (micro-cap); stock at ~$3.33 as of Feb 2026
- Restructuring/severance costs in both 2024 ($1.8M) and 2025 ($1.3M YTD) confirm ongoing workforce adjustments

## Red Flags
- **Going concern language** in 2024 10-K is a serious signal - company is in financial distress; survival depends on Commerce Media growth materializing
- **Consecutive revenue declines** across 2023, 2024 and through 2025 (even accounting for intentional legacy wind-down)
- **Micro-cap public company** with ~$50M market cap and negative EBITDA - acquisition target or delisting risk is non-trivial
- **Small tech team** (60 people) handling broad stack - likely high context load per engineer and manager
- Glassdoor reviews mention layoffs without warning and low career opportunity scores - consistent with ongoing restructuring
- No engineering blog or open source presence suggests low investment in employer brand and external technical credibility
