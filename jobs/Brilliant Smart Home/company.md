# Brilliant Smart Home

- **Company Type:** Product (smart home hardware + software platform)
- **Stage:** Post-bankruptcy restart, privately held, early-stage recovery
- **Size:** ~27 employees (15 product + tech)
- **Remote Policy:** Hybrid, 3 days/week in office (San Mateo, CA), flexible on which days

## Quick Take
- The company ran out of money and laid off its entire staff in May 2024 after failing to close a Series C, then sold in an asset sale to private investors in August 2024. It is now a small, restarted company rebuilding from near-zero.
- Very small engineering org (~15 product + tech). Business model pivoted away from retail (Amazon, Best Buy, Home Depot) toward direct sales and professional installers/builders.
- Fresh $9.7M raise in September 2025 from existing/related investors, tied to launching gen-2 hardware and AI features. Financial runway and stability are still unproven at this stage.

## Milestones
- 2015/2016: Founded in San Mateo, CA. Raised $64M in venture funding over its lifetime for an in-wall smart home control panel system.
- May 2024: Ran out of cash after a failed Series C, laid off the entire staff, ceased new sales, and began an asset sale process.
- August 2024: Acquired out of bankruptcy by private investors (Almeida Strategic Investments and Cullinan Holdings, led by Evan and Michael Almeida with David Blum) — relaunched as "Brilliant NextGen."
- Late 2024–H1 2025: Missed initial relaunch deadline, then shipped second-generation hardware (faster processors, higher-resolution displays).
- September 2025: Secured $9.7M in new funding (Almeida Strategic Investments and Tyrod Taylor's Strategic Investments Fund) to fund a second-gen Power-over-Ethernet product, partner network expansion, and AI-driven features.

## Company & Product
Brilliant makes an in-wall smart home control panel (touchscreen + voice) that replaces a standard light switch and controls third-party smart devices (Sonos, Nest, Ring, Philips Hue, etc.), plus smart dimmer switches and plugs, all tied together by a mobile app. Post-restructuring, the go-to-market shifted from consumer retail to single-family home builders, multifamily developers, and professional smart-home installers — a B2B2C model rather than direct-to-consumer.

## Engineering Culture
Public information is thin given the company's small size and recent relaunch — no engineering blog or GitHub presence found. Job postings describe small, cross-functional teams (engineering alongside product, design, and data). Prior-era (pre-bankruptcy) Glassdoor reviews described "incredible engineers, exceptional talent" and a "relaxed, welcoming culture" alongside sharply negative comments about senior leadership ("grossly mismanaged," "no drive to move fast or focus," blaming poor technical architecture on user error). These reviews predate the 2024 collapse and full leadership/ownership change, so their relevance to the current NextGen organization is uncertain.

- Client/embedded: Full-stack roles use Python plus QML/QT and C++ for embedded panel UI and firmware
- No public engineering blog or open-source presence found

## Team Health
- No current (post-relaunch) Glassdoor engineering reviews were accessible (Glassdoor engineering page returned a 403; general company reviews reflect the pre-bankruptcy era).
- Pre-bankruptcy reviews were mixed: strong individual engineering talent and a collaborative day-to-day environment, but recurring complaints about senior leadership competence and lack of strategic focus ("does 5 things exceptionally mediocre").
- Current leadership is materially different (new ownership since August 2024), so pre-2024 culture/leadership reviews should be weighted cautiously.

## Business Stability
- Went through a full company failure and asset-sale bankruptcy in mid-2024 after $64M in lifetime VC funding and a failed Series C. This is a genuine business-continuity red flag, not a rumor.
- Revived under new private ownership with a $9.7M raise in September 2025 — meaningfully smaller than pre-bankruptcy funding rounds, and from a narrow set of investors (Almeida Strategic Investments, Tyrod Taylor's fund) rather than a broad VC syndicate.
- Broader smart-home VC market is weak: 2024 was reportedly the lowest year for smart-home/smart-building investment in a decade (under $100M industry-wide), and comparable companies (View, Veev) also failed or were sold off in distress. This is a structurally hard sector to fund right now.
- Company size today is very small (~27 total, ~15 in product/tech), consistent with an early-stage restart rather than a stable scale-up.

## Red Flags
- Company shut down entirely and laid off all staff in May 2024; current entity is a post-bankruptcy relaunch under new ownership, roughly one year old as "Brilliant NextGen."
- Small, recent funding round ($9.7M) relative to the company's cash burn history and prior raises ($64M lifetime pre-collapse) — runway and growth trajectory are unproven.
- Historical (pre-restructure) reviews flagged serious senior leadership and strategic-focus problems; current leadership is new, so this can't be confirmed as resolved or recurring without direct verification (e.g., in interviews).
- Sector-wide headwinds: smart-home VC funding is at a decade low and multiple peer companies (View, Veev) have also failed or been sold in distress in the same period.
