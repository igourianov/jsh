# PointClickCare

- **Company Type:** SaaS / Healthcare IT Platform
- **Stage:** Late-stage private, PE-backed (Enterprise scale)
- **Size:** ~2,300-2,800 employees
- **Remote Policy:** Not clearly published; roles observed to be hybrid/onsite around Mississauga/Toronto HQ, some remote-eligible postings exist

## Quick Take
- Dominant EHR/health record platform for the long-term and post-acute care (LTPAC) market, serving 27,000+ providers, 2,800+ hospitals/health systems and 2,000+ ambulatory clinics
- Financially stable and still acquisitive (7 acquisitions to date), but engineering reviews describe slow release cadence (quarterly deploys, ~5-week sprints) and heavy micromanagement
- Healthcare mission resonates with staff, but "optics over outcomes" culture and comp-below-market complaints are recurring themes worth probing in interviews

## Milestones
- 2000: Founded in Mississauga, Ontario by brothers Mike and Dave Wessinger
- 2021: Minority investment from Dragoneer Investment Group and Hellman & Friedman values the company at $4B
- Later 2021/2022: Valuation reported to rise to ~$5B as Hellman & Friedman and JMI Equity increase stakes via secondary share purchases
- 2022: Acquisition activity continues (e.g. Audacious Inquiry) expanding into acute/ambulatory care data exchange
- 2024: Launches new staffing-focused products for senior care providers
- Jan 2026: Launches AI Referral Advisor for skilled nursing facilities to speed admissions
- Jun 2026: Launches Advisor Suite, expanding AI-native workflow automation

## Company & Product
PointClickCare is a health-tech company providing cloud-based electronic health record (EHR) and care management software for the senior care and post-acute care industry, plus data-exchange tools connecting LTPAC providers with acute care hospitals and payers. It has grown through both product expansion and acquisition (American HealthTech, Patient Pattern, Audacious Inquiry among others) and is increasingly positioning itself around AI-native workflow automation (referral management, advisor tools) layered on its core EHR platform.

## Engineering Culture
Company blog content (Meet Our Engineering Team) emphasizes mission-driven, people-first messaging: "Inspiring. Rewarding. Balanced. Innovative." Engineers quoted describe an inclusive environment and cross-functional impact on systems that affect "millions" of patients. However, this is marketing copy rather than an independent engineering blog — no technical blog posts, open-source presence, or public engineering practices (testing, CI/CD, architecture write-ups) were found. That absence, combined with Glassdoor reports of quarterly deployments and 5-week sprints, suggests engineering practices are more traditional/waterfall-adjacent than a typical modern SaaS shop.

## Tech Stack
- Backend/framework: ASP.NET / C#
- Infrastructure: AWS (HIPAA-compliant), Google Cloud Platform, Kubernetes, NGINX, Jenkins
- Frontend/marketing site: WordPress, Bootstrap, jQuery, Modernizr
- Source control/CI: Atlassian Bitbucket, Jenkins
- No public engineering blog detailing architecture decisions was found; stack info is sourced from aggregator sites (RocketReach, StackShare) rather than PointClickCare itself, so treat with moderate confidence

## Team Health
- Glassdoor: Software Engineer rating 3.3/5 (44 reviews); Senior Software Engineer rating 3.6/5 (33 reviews)
- Positives cited: strong coworkers, good work-life balance, meaningful mission-driven work
- Concerns cited: management described as "terrible" with "no clear vision for the product," heavy micromanagement, culture "rewards optics over outcomes," poor engineering design with sprints near 5 weeks and deployments roughly quarterly, compensation not aligned with market, declining morale, references to "constant rounds of layoffs" in some reviews

## Business Stability
- Total funding raised: ~$3.62B; last reported valuation ~$4-5B (2021-2022, private equity-backed by Hellman & Friedman, JMI Equity, Dragoneer)
- Privately held, no confirmed IPO plans
- Employee count roughly flat to slightly growing in 2023-2024 (reports show modest declines and increases depending on source/quarter), active job postings dropped over 50% in 2024 vs 2023, suggesting hiring slowdown
- Continues to acquire and launch new AI products in 2026, indicating ongoing investment despite slower hiring
- Some Glassdoor reviews reference periodic layoffs/resizing tied to individual performance reviews rather than one large announced event; no major layoff headline found in search

## Red Flags
- Recurring Glassdoor complaints about micromanagement, unclear product vision, and "optics over outcomes" culture
- Slow release cadence (quarterly deployments, long sprints) is unusual for a company this size and may indicate technical debt or process dysfunction
- Compensation reported as below market by multiple reviewers
- No independent, technical engineering blog or open-source presence to verify claimed practices — culture content found is HR/marketing-authored
- Reduced job posting volume in 2024 and mentions of periodic layoffs warrant direct questions about team stability and headcount plans in interviews
