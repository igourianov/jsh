- **Company Type:** SaaS/Platform (Contract Lifecycle Management)
- **Stage:** Scale-up (PE-backed, KKR majority acquisition May 2024)

- **Remote Policy:** Remote-first; positions posted across US and Canada

## Quick Take
- Legitimate market leader in CLM with 6 consecutive Gartner Magic Quadrant Leader placements and 95%+ customer retention

## Company & Product
Agiloft provides a Data-first Agreement Platform (DAP) centered on Contract Lifecycle Management. Their platform is a low-code/no-code configuration environment with pre-built modules for contract management, IT service management and business process automation. Key differentiators include a 99.6% implementation success rate, 95%+ customer retention, and 98.5% of customers going live under budget. The company serves 900+ global enterprise customers across multiple industries. Revenue reported at ~$71.4M in 2024 with 1,500+ customers. Product strategy is now heavily AI-focused under the "AI on the Inside" initiative, with generative AI capabilities for clause analysis, risk flagging, document Q&A and obligation management.

## Engineering Culture
- No dedicated public engineering blog; blog content is product/marketing focused
- GitLab CI used for version control and CI/CD pipelines
- Glassdoor engineering reviews mention exposure to modern infrastructure tooling (AWS, Terraform, Datadog, Linux) with meaningful ownership of infrastructure work
- Areas of friction: management culture slows execution, ownership and decision rights can be unclear, inconsistent standards for Terraform/CI/CD and alerting across teams
- Company philosophy "EX=CX" (Employee Experience = Customer Experience) - suggests genuine investment in culture
- Meeting-free Fridays and quarterly wellness days indicate awareness of focus time needs

- No reported layoffs in 2024 or 2025
- Strong ARR growth; enterprise customer base expanded 30% in last reported year
- 250% increase in AI feature adoption signals product-market fit for new AI capabilities
- International expansion underway (EMEA, APAC)

## Red Flags
- KKR PE ownership introduces exit pressure (IPO or resale within 5-7 years) which may drive aggressive growth targets over engineering quality
- Codebase dates to 1991 - likely significant legacy tech debt under the modern tooling surface
- AngularJS in the stack suggests frontend modernization may be incomplete or ongoing
- Inconsistent engineering standards across teams (noted in Glassdoor reviews) is a concern for an EM role - may mean inheriting fragmented practices
- Small engineering team (~330 total employees across all functions) means the eng org is probably 100-150 people; scope may be narrower than enterprise-scale roles
