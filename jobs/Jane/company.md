# Jane

- **Company Type:** SaaS / Practice Management Platform
- **Stage:** Scale-up (bootstrapped, $1.8B valuation)
- **Size:** ~650 employees
- **Remote Policy:** Remote-first (all roles remote, anywhere in Canada)

## Quick Take
- Rare bootstrapped unicorn in healthcare SaaS. Profitable since founding, ~US$100M ARR, no VC or PE control.
- Founder-led with strong values alignment ("Help the Helpers"). Avoided layoffs during 2022 downturn and gave raises instead.
- Engineering is scaling rapidly with acknowledged growing pains. Tech debt and management consistency are active concerns per Glassdoor reviews.

## Milestones
- **2012** - Founded by Alison Taylor and Trevor Johnston in North Vancouver, BC
- **2022** - Avoided layoffs during tech downturn, gave employee raises
- **2025 (May)** - Hit $1.8B valuation via $500M+ secondary deal (TCV, JMI Equity, Tidemark Management buying shares from existing investors and employees)
- **2025** - Ranked #308 on Deloitte Technology Fast 500, #15 on Deloitte Canada Enterprise Industry Leaders
- **2026** - 200,000+ practitioners across 50,000 clinics in Canada, U.S. and U.K.

## Company & Product
Jane is a cloud-based practice management and EMR platform built for allied health clinics (physiotherapy, massage therapy, chiropractic, mental health counselling, midwifery). The platform consolidates online booking, charting, scheduling, telehealth, secure payments and insurance billing into one system.

Target market is small and medium-sized multi-discipline health clinics. Pricing is per-provider (~$54 USD/month), transparent with no setup fees. Growth has been primarily word-of-mouth, which is notable for a company approaching $100M ARR.

The product competes with SimplePractice (more mental health focused) and other clinic management tools. Jane differentiates through its interdisciplinary clinic support and strong customer satisfaction (high Capterra ratings).

Currently investing in AI features including an "AI scribe" that generates clinical notes during patient visits.

## Engineering Culture
Ruby on Rails monolith on the backend, with React and Vue.js on the frontend. The engineering org has doubled in size recently.

**Positives:**
- Strong ownership culture. Teams feel collective responsibility for what they build.
- Test coverage is taken seriously given millions of end users depend on the platform.
- Autonomy for teams to stay connected to customer needs and chart their own course.
- Investing in AI tooling for both the product and internal development practices (AI-assisted coding, MCP integration).

**Concerns:**
- Significant tech debt acknowledged by multiple reviewers.
- Engineering practices are still maturing. Release process described as "painfully slow and manual" by some.
- Management consistency issues. Different managers have "very different approaches."
- Some reviews cite top-down tool decisions made without developer input.
- One notable Glassdoor review titled "Good people, poor senior engineering leadership."

**Open Source:**
- 30 public repositories on GitHub (github.com/janeapp).
- Notable: "riffer" (Ruby framework for AI-powered applications), "fast-mcp" (Ruby MCP implementation).
- Forks of Twilio video, Jitsi (video chat), Claude Code Action, Storybook MCP.

**Themes from reviews:**

- Remote-first culture is well-supported with in-person retreats and departmental get-togethers.
- Company has been hiring experienced engineers and engineering leaders to raise the technical bar.

## Business Stability
**Strong.** Jane is bootstrapped, profitable, and approaching $100M ARR with only ~$10M in primary funding ever raised. The $1.8B valuation secondary deal in 2025 provided liquidity for employees and early investors without diluting founder control. No VC board seats or pressure to grow unsustainably.

The healthcare practice management market is stable and growing. Jane's word-of-mouth growth and high customer retention suggest strong product-market fit. No layoffs in company history.

## Red Flags
- Tech debt is a recurring theme. Multiple Glassdoor reviews mention it as a significant challenge.
- Engineering leadership quality is inconsistent. At least one review specifically calls out "poor senior engineering leadership."
- Release process has been described as slow and manual. This could indicate immature DevOps practices despite using CircleCI.
- Rapid engineering org growth (doubled recently) without fully mature processes could mean a chaotic environment.
- Management consistency is a work in progress. The company acknowledges this but it's still an active issue.

None of these are dealbreakers. They are typical scale-up growing pains, and the company appears self-aware about them.
