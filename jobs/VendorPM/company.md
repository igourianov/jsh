# VendorPM

- **Company Type:** SaaS / PropTech Platform
- **Stage:** Scale-up (Series A)
- **Size:** ~70-100 employees, ~15 in engineering
- **Remote Policy:** Hybrid with fully remote option. Office in downtown Toronto. Teams meet in person roughly once a week. Remote applicants must reside in Canada.

## Quick Take
- PropTech niche with real traction: 10,000+ buildings, 70,000+ vendors. Solving a genuine pain point in commercial real estate vendor management.
- Glassdoor reviews raise serious concerns about engineering and product leadership. Blame culture, micromanagement and lack of genuine technical direction are recurring themes.
- Financially stable post-Series A ($26M total raised) with no reported layoffs, but no new funding since 2022. Runway and path to profitability are worth probing.

## Milestones
- **2020:** Founded in Toronto by Emiel Bril, Dylan Sher and Neel Bali. Launched with 1,000 users and secured pre-seed financing.
- **2021:** Grew from 2 to 70+ employees; won Canadian Proptech Innovation Award
- **2022 (Feb):** Raised $6M seed round
- **2022 (Aug):** Closed $20M Series A led by Bessemer Venture Partners; expanded to US (Chicago); won BOMA Toronto Pinnacle Award in Innovation
- **2023-2025:** Great Place to Work certified three consecutive years
- **2025 (Mar):** Launched Contract Management module, creating end-to-end vendor lifecycle management platform

## Company & Product
VendorPM is a PropTech SaaS platform that modernizes how property managers and vendors work together. The platform covers the full vendor lifecycle: credentialing, compliance management, eBidding/RFX workflows and contract management with ERP integration.

The company targets commercial real estate firms and property management groups. Their marketplace connects 70,000+ vendors with 10,000+ buildings. CEO Emiel Bril started a window cleaning business at 14 and experienced the industry's reliance on email, spreadsheets and word-of-mouth firsthand.

Core values: Focus, Elevate, Own It, Customer Obsession.

## Engineering Culture
The engineering team is approximately 15 people, led by a VP of Engineering (Apurav Chauhan). VendorPM has a GitHub organization with at least one public TypeScript repository (vendorPM-chat). No dedicated engineering blog was found.

The company is exploring AI/ML capabilities and "agentic procurement" as a product direction, which suggests investment in newer technologies. They are evaluating when to extend their TypeScript stack versus introducing Python for AI/ML workloads. Engineers are expected to leverage AI-assisted development tools (Cursor, Claude, Copilot) to accelerate their work.

Glassdoor reviews paint a concerning picture of the engineering culture: engineers report being discouraged from contributing to product ideation, limited cross-functional collaboration between Product and Engineering, and leadership decisions described as "performative rather than thoughtful."

## Business Stability
- **Total Funding:** $26M across 3 rounds (latest: $20M Series A in Aug 2022)
- **Lead Investor:** Bessemer Venture Partners
- **No reported layoffs** as of early 2026
- **No new funding rounds** since 2022. The 3.5+ year gap since Series A without a Series B could indicate either approaching profitability or difficulty raising the next round.
- Active product development continues (Contract Management launch in 2025)

## Red Flags
- **Engineering leadership concerns:** Multiple Glassdoor reviews cite the CTO as lacking competence, ignoring technical input and defaulting to buzzwords. The VP Engineering role was recently posted, which could signal leadership turnover or growth.
- **Blame culture:** Reviews describe a pattern of blame-seeking rather than collaborative problem-solving. Mistakes lead to replacement rather than learning.
- **Micromanagement:** Leadership reportedly prevents teams from implementing long-term roadmaps, instead redirecting focus to whatever problem leadership is fixated on that week.
- **Product-Engineering disconnect:** Engineers are discouraged from contributing to ideation. Product teams are not empowered to collaborate deeply with Engineering.
- **Compensation:** Below-average rating (3.1/5) on Glassdoor for a Toronto-based tech company.
- **Funding gap:** No new funding since August 2022. Worth understanding burn rate and path to profitability.
