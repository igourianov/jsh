# Phreesia

- **Company Type:** SaaS / Healthcare Platform
- **Stage:** Public (NYSE: PHR)
- **Size:** ~1,400 employees (US and Canada)
- **Remote Policy:** Distributed/remote-friendly, with clusters in NY, Raleigh, Ottawa; fully remote roles exist but remote workers report weaker support than office clusters

## Quick Take
- Engineering-specific reviews are strong (4.5/5 on Glassdoor for Software Engineer role, above industry average) but company-wide rating is much weaker (3.1/5), suggesting engineering is a relatively good pocket in a company with broader organizational problems.
- Currently navigating a rough patch: March 2026 guidance cut tanked the stock 26.6% in a day, triggering a securities fraud class action, followed by a May 2026 restructuring cutting ~220 roles (half contractor).
- Recurring reviewer complaints across the company: low job security, high turnover, weak promotion/pay-raise structure, no 401k match.

## Milestones
- Founded January 2005 by CEO Chaim Indig and COO Evan Roberts; Michael Weintraub chairman since inception.
- July 18, 2019: IPO on NYSE (PHR).
- 2021: Acquired QueueDr (scheduling), rebranded Appointment Accelerator.
- December 2021: Acquired Insignia Health (Patient Activation Measure licensing).
- August 2023: Acquired Access eForms (electronic forms automation, Sulphur Springs TX).
- AccessOne acquisition referenced in 2026 growth strategy (network solutions segment) - now central to a securities fraud complaint alleging overstated growth prospects.
- March 30, 2026: Cut FY2027 revenue growth guidance citing "worsening visibility" and weaker pharma marketing commitments in Network Solutions; stock fell 26.6% ($3.03) to $8.38/share in one day.
- May 7, 2026: Restructuring plan eliminating ~220 positions (roughly half contractor roles) to align costs with business priorities; company says savings were already reflected in prior guidance.
- 2026: Facing an active securities fraud class action (multiple law firms soliciting shareholders), deadline activity through July 2026, alleging management overstated Network Solutions/AccessOne growth while concealing slowing demand.

## Company & Product
Phreesia provides a SaaS patient intake and engagement platform: registration, scheduling, payments and clinical support tools used by healthcare organizations, processing 150M+ patient visits annually. Headquartered in Wilmington, DE. Revenue approximately $481M (FY ending Jan 31, 2026). The role under consideration sits on the VoiceAI product team, building AI-powered voice solutions for patient interactions - a newer, forward-looking product line inside a company otherwise dealing with growth-guidance and cost-cutting pressure.

## Engineering Culture
No public engineering blog found, and the GitHub org (github.com/Phreesia) has no visible public repositories or members - engineering work appears to happen entirely behind closed doors, no open-source presence to evaluate. Software Engineer-specific Glassdoor reviews (4.5/5, 17 reviews) describe a culture of shipping code to tangible products, meaningful contributions from day one, and engineers included in product discussions. Distributed teams are noted as presenting communication challenges. Given the small review sample size (17), treat this rating with some caution.

## Tech Stack
Reported stack: C#/.NET, SQL Server, MongoDB, Redis. Enterprise/cloud tooling includes AppDynamics, Salesforce (Force.com), AWS, DocuSign, Red Hat. The job posting for this specific role (Manager, Software Engineering - VoiceAI) lists .NET (C#) or Java, MySQL, DynamoDB, AWS, React/Angular, and an explicit mandate to drive Voice AI/LLM and AI-assisted development adoption.

## Team Health
- Software Engineer reviews: 4.5/5 overall and 4.5/5 for comp/benefits (small sample, 17 reviews), 31% above Healthcare industry average.
- Company-wide: 3.1/5 overall on Glassdoor (924 reviews), 3.7/5 on Blind (41 reviews).
- Work-life balance rated 3.1/5 company-wide.
- Recurring themes: career advancement and promotion structure complaints, management practice concerns, job security anxiety ("very frequent changes and no job security"), turnover ("constantly hiring and firing with massive turnover"), no 401k match, remote workers report weaker support/inclusion versus NY/Raleigh/Ottawa office clusters.
- Positive notes: good pay, genuinely remote-friendly for some roles, decent benefits mentioned by some reviewers.

## Business Stability
Public company (NYSE: PHR), FY revenue ~$481M. Raised $109M pre-IPO from Polaris Partners, Sandbox Industries, LLR Partners. March 2026 guidance cut and 26.6% single-day stock drop signal real deterioration in the Network Solutions (pharma marketing) segment. Active securities fraud class action alleging management misrepresented growth prospects tied to Network Solutions and the AccessOne acquisition. May 2026 restructuring cut ~220 roles (half contractor) - a meaningful cut relative to ~1,400 total headcount (roughly 15%), though company frames it as already priced into guidance rather than a new shock.

## Red Flags
- Active securities fraud class action naming the company and executives - a serious governance/credibility signal, not just routine market noise.
- 26.6% single-day stock collapse in March 2026 followed two months later by a ~220-role restructuring: this is a company under real financial pressure, not a stable scale-up.
- Company-wide Glassdoor/Blind scores meaningfully lower than the engineering-specific score, with recurring complaints about job security, turnover, and promotion/pay structure - worth probing directly in interviews, especially given contractor-heavy cuts and a mandate on this specific role to manage contractors/vendors.
- No visible public engineering blog or open-source activity, limiting external verification of engineering practices beyond employee reviews.
- Small sample size (17) for the positive Software Engineer-specific Glassdoor rating - encouraging but not statistically strong.
