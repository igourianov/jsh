# Maximus Canada

- **Company Type:** IT Services / Government Services (Product line: Medigent eHealth)
- **Stage:** Enterprise (subsidiary of NYSE-listed Maximus Inc.)
- **Size:** 501-1000 employees on LinkedIn (some aggregators list about 391)
- **Remote Policy:** Remote-first across Canada

## Quick Take
- Canadian arm of a large US public government-services contractor. Engineering is a small product unit (Medigent) inside a business dominated by contact centres, employment services and consulting.
- Medigent is a long-lived healthcare platform (claims, drug information system, HL7v3, CPhA 3.0) sold to provincial governments. Expect legacy modernization and long public-sector contract cycles.
- Concern: Glassdoor is mediocre (3.2 in Canada, 45% recommend company-wide) and the posted pay is modest for a Director.

## Milestones
- 1975: Maximus Inc. founded in the US, later listed on NYSE (MMS), HQ Tysons, Virginia.
- 2002: Maximus Canada incorporated in British Columbia. Acquired Themis Program Management & Consulting, which ran the BC Family Maintenance Enforcement Program until 2019.
- 2011: Selected to implement the Medigent Drug Information System for Nova Scotia.
- Expanded from BC to federal, provincial and municipal programs across Canada, with an Ottawa office beside the Victoria HQ.
- 2021: Parent acquired the federal student loan servicing contract (Aidvantage) from Navient.
- 2026: Named one of BC's Top Employers. Parent raised FY2026 EPS guidance to $8.25-8.55 and authorized a $400M buyback.

## Company & Product
Maximus Canada delivers public programs in three areas: customer experience and contact centre services, technology and consulting, and health services. The health line includes Medigent, an eHealth suite covering claims and payment modules (medical, pharmacy, supplementary benefits, dental, medical travel, reciprocal billing), prior authorization workflows and a Drug Information System that stores prescriptions and dispensing events and runs real-time drug utilization review. It integrates with pharmacy point-of-service systems and physician EMRs. Customers are Canadian provincial governments and some international clients. Headquartered in Victoria, BC, with an Ottawa office.

## Engineering Culture
No public engineering blog, open source presence or tech talks found. Per the posting, the org is a matrix (Product Management, Architecture, QA, DevOps, Support, Professional Services, Security, Privacy) and is pushing AI-assisted development and DevOps practices. Government delivery implies formal governance, compliance and contractual SLAs. Portfolio mixes legacy solutions and newer SaaS.

## Tech Stack
Not published. Public material shows HL7v3 messaging, CPhA 3.0 and NeCST pharmacy claims messaging. The posting mentions multi-tenant, multi-jurisdiction configurable platforms and cloud transformation. Specific languages and frameworks are unknown.

## Team Health
- Glassdoor, Canada: 3.2/5 from about 128 reviews. Work-life balance 3.5, compensation 3.2.
- Company-wide: 45% would recommend, from about 5,300 reviews.
- Themes: flexible and remote work and reasonable pay, against complaints about management quality and workplace culture. Many reviews relate to contact centre roles rather than engineering.

## Business Stability
Parent is profitable and public. Q2 FY2026 revenue was $1.31B versus $1.36B a year earlier, with EBITDA margin guidance raised to about 14.2%. Heavy dependence on government contracts means exposure to contract loss and policy change, but Canadian provincial health contracts tend to be long. Small layoff reports exist at the parent level (34 employees). Canada-specific stability looks fine.

## Red Flags
- Posted pay 155-185K CAD with no bonus or equity, low for a Director.
- Weak Glassdoor scores and management-quality complaints.
- Legacy portfolio and public-sector pace. Engineering is a minor unit of a services company, so product investment may be limited.
- No visible engineering brand or stack transparency.
