# Autodesk

- **Company Type:** SaaS / Product (design and engineering software, shifting to cloud platform + industry clouds)
- **Stage:** Public (NASDAQ: ADSK), mature enterprise
- **Size:** ~13,000-14,000 employees (after multiple rounds of cuts; ~14,600 pre-2026 reductions)
- **Remote Policy:** Hybrid / flexible. Many engineering roles are remote-friendly, but expectations vary by team and location.

## Quick Take
- Stable, profitable, category-leading software vendor (AutoCAD, Revit, Fusion, Maya) with a strong recurring-revenue moat. Low business-failure risk.
- Active platform and AI pivot (Autodesk Platform Services, industry clouds, 3D foundation models). Good story for an EM who wants to work on cloud/platform modernization.
- Concern: repeated annual layoffs (2023, Feb 2025, Jan 2026) signal ongoing reorg churn. Most cuts are sales/GTM, not engineering, but the pattern affects morale and org stability.

## Milestones
- 1982: Founded by John Walker and 12 others; AutoCAD ships and becomes the dominant CAD product.
- 1985: IPO on NASDAQ.
- 2014-2016: Transition from perpetual licenses to subscription model (a multi-year, painful revenue transition that ultimately succeeded).
- 2018-2020: Major construction push via Assemble, PlanGrid and BuildingConnected acquisitions; consolidated into Autodesk Construction Cloud.
- 2020-2022: Rebrand of Forge to Autodesk Platform Services (APS); push toward cloud data platform.
- Feb 2025: ~9% workforce reduction (~1,350 roles), tied to GTM transformation and reinvestment in AI/platform.
- Jan 2026: ~7% workforce reduction (~1,000 roles), mostly customer-facing sales, completing the multi-year GTM transformation. Pretax charges $135M-$160M; plan to conclude by end of FY2027.

## Company & Product
Autodesk makes design, engineering and entertainment software for architecture/engineering/construction (AEC), manufacturing (Fusion, Inventor), and media & entertainment (Maya, 3ds Max, Flow). Business model is subscription SaaS with very high recurring revenue and strong renewal economics. The company is in the late stages of a "go-to-market transformation" (moving to a direct/agency billing model and annual billing) and is investing heavily in a unified cloud data platform and AI features layered on its parametric/physics-based 3D engines.

Q1 FY2027 (reported May 2026): revenue $1.93B, up 18% YoY; non-GAAP operating margin 39%; free cash flow $876M (up 58%); FY2027 revenue guidance raised to ~$8.16B-$8.22B. Financially healthy and growing.

## Engineering Culture
- Public-facing engineering presence is solid: tech.autodesk.com (research, data science, software stories), the Autodesk Developer Blog, and the Autodesk Platform Services (APS, formerly Forge) developer community. Signals real investment in developer experience and external API ecosystem.
- Internal Developer Platform (IDP) with SDLC visibility/telemetry; teams run hundreds of thousands of builds/month across thousands of configurations. Indicates mature build/CI infrastructure and platform-engineering maturity, relevant for a "Developer Productivity & AI Systems" type role.
- Agile delivery, cross-functional teams. Large-org dynamics apply: process overhead, coordination across many product lines.
- AI direction is concrete (3D foundation models, geometry-aware models) rather than purely marketing, which is a positive signal for engineering work that is not just LLM wrapping.

## Tech Stack
- **Desktop/core products:** large C++ codebases (AutoCAD, Revit, Maya etc.) - legacy and substantial.
- **Cloud/web:** React, Node.js, REST APIs; Java/Spring Boot microservices on some teams; AWS as primary cloud; databases include SQL and NoSQL (MongoDB referenced).
- **Platform:** Autodesk Platform Services (APS) - web service APIs/SDKs for design and engineering data interchange; industry cloud data model.
- Architecture is a mix: modernizing toward cloud microservices and a unified data platform while maintaining heavyweight desktop applications. An EM should expect both greenfield cloud work and integration with legacy systems depending on the team.

## Team Health
- Glassdoor overall ~4.0-4.1/5; ~80% would recommend to a friend. Work-life balance rated ~4.1 company-wide.
- Senior Software Engineers rate ~3.8-4.1; engineering side generally reports good WLB and flexibility.
- Experience varies sharply by division and team. Some reviews praise true WLB and "whole person" culture; others cite overload and the "usual struggles of a large org" (bureaucracy, slow decisions, reorg fatigue).
- Net: healthy but heterogeneous. Diligence on the specific team, manager and product line matters more than the company average.

## Business Stability
- Strongly profitable, high free cash flow, durable subscription revenue, raised guidance. Near-zero risk of financial distress.
- The recurring layoffs are restructuring/efficiency-driven and reinvestment-focused (sales/GTM), not survival-driven. CEO Anagnost stated the Jan 2026 cut is not driven by external economic pressure and "will not become an annual process," though the company has now cut in 2023, 2025 and 2026.

## Red Flags
- Repeated annual workforce reductions (2023, 2025, 2026). Even if engineering is less affected, the pattern creates reorg churn, morale drag and uncertainty. Ask directly about team stability and recent attrition.
- Legacy C++ desktop codebases carry inherent tech debt; modernization is real but incremental. Expect a mix of exciting platform work and heavy legacy maintenance depending on the org.
- Large-org friction: cross-product coordination, process overhead, variable team experience. The "go-to-market transformation" has been long and disruptive.
- For a hands-on EM, validate how much of the role is genuine platform/AI building vs. maintaining mature products.
