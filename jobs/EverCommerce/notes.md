### Screening question answers submitted

**Q: List out at least two of our company values and describe how you would incorporate these into future positions.**

Two values that resonate with me are quality, ownership and passion.

Quality. To me, quality isn't a checkbox at the end of a sprint. It's the product actually succeeding for customers. I'd keep the team focused on that outcome: building the right thing, shipping it reliably and measuring whether it moves the needle for contractors. Quality is the goal, not a tax on velocity.

Ownership. Ownership can't be mandated, it has to be grown. I'd instill it by treating engineers with respect and giving them a clear vision of the product goals so they understand the "why" behind the work. When people own the outcome and not just their tickets, they make better calls and hold a higher bar without being told to.

Passion. I lead with a learning mindset and bring it to the team. That means staying curious, evaluating and adopting new technology, and putting AI-augmented development to work where it raises the bar. For me passion ties straight back to quality: the drive to keep improving how we build is what keeps the product excellent over time.

**Q: What is your definition of success in this role?**

Success in this role comes down to two things: the product winning and the team being able to keep it winning.

Product success. I measure myself by outcomes for customers, not output. For Joist that means the squads are shipping features that contractors actually adopt and rely on, delivered reliably and on a predictable cadence. If we're moving the metrics that matter to the business and to the people using the product, the work is succeeding.

A high-performing team. The product only stays successful if the team can sustain it. Success is two squads that own their outcomes, ship independently without stepping on each other, and hold a high quality bar on their own. It's engineers growing into bigger roles, low attrition because people want to stay, and a delivery process that's fast without burning anyone out.

The two reinforce each other. A healthy, motivated team is what makes consistent product wins possible, and real product impact is what keeps a team engaged. My job is to set the vision, remove roadblocks and create the conditions for both.
