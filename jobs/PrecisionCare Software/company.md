# PrecisionCare Software

- **Company Type:** SaaS / Product (vertical market EHR)
- **Stage:** Small, privately-held, recently acquired by a PE-style holding company
- **Size:** ~11-50 employees (estimates range 15-40)
- **Remote Policy:** Unclear from public sources; HQ in Hurley/New Paltz, NY, historically appears to be an on-site/local shop

## Quick Take
- Tiny, decades-old niche EHR vendor for IDD (Intellectual and Developmental Disabilities) and Behavioral Health providers, not a high-growth tech company
- Just acquired (Feb 2025) by EXA Capital, a "permanent hold" vertical-market-software investor - a classic PE buy-and-hold of a legacy niche software business
- Very thin public footprint: almost no engineering blog, no GitHub presence, one Glassdoor review, tech stack skews dated (jQuery, AngularJS, VBScript)

## Milestones
- 1999: Founded by a team of human service providers and software developers
- 2025-02-24: Acquired by EXA Capital (Dallas-based vertical market software investor founded 2020); existing management (President Christine Casillo) stays in place, deal terms undisclosed

## Company & Product
PrecisionCare builds a mobile-first, cloud-based EHR/case-record-management platform for frontline human service providers, targeting IDD case management, behavioral health documentation, clinic records, and TBI case management. Value proposition is reducing paperwork burden and ensuring state/federal regulatory compliance for a narrow, compliance-heavy vertical. This is a mature, narrow-niche product rather than a growth-stage startup.

## Engineering Culture
No engineering blog, no visible open-source activity, no GitHub org found. Public information about engineering practices is essentially absent - a red flag in itself for visibility into how the team operates. Job postings mention entry-level/internship roles doing web and mobile development, suggesting a small, generalist engineering team rather than specialized platform teams.

## Tech Stack
Historical job postings reference: C#, JavaScript, HTML5, CSS, jQuery, AngularJS, SQL, VBScript, PHP, WordPress/WooCommerce (marketing site). This points to an older, likely partially-legacy .NET-era stack with pre-modern-framework front-end tooling (AngularJS rather than Angular/React), consistent with a long-lived niche product that has accreted rather than been rearchitected.

## Team Health
- Only 1 Glassdoor review is publicly indexed (page itself returned 403 on direct fetch, so details are second-hand via search snippets)
- Search-derived snippet suggests a small, friendly, low-pressure environment with decent work-life balance, but this is based on a single data point and should be treated with caution
- Compensation & benefits rating cited around 3/5 stars, again based on minimal sample size

## Business Stability
- Bootstrapped/independent for ~25 years before acquisition
- Acquired Feb 2025 by EXA Capital, which explicitly follows a permanent-hold (no forced exit) strategy for enterprise vertical-market software - generally a more stable ownership model than typical PE roll-up-and-flip, but still introduces new financial ownership, board oversight, and potential pressure to professionalize/cut costs
- No layoffs, funding rounds, or other negative news found
- Very small company size (~15-50 employees) means limited scale, limited internal mobility, and higher key-person risk

## Red Flags
- Extremely thin public information across the board (engineering blog, Glassdoor, GitHub) makes it hard to verify culture or technical practices independently
- Tech stack references (AngularJS, VBScript, jQuery) suggest meaningful legacy code and possible tech debt
- Recent change of ownership (Feb 2025) adds uncertainty - even under a "permanent hold" thesis, new PE-style ownership often brings changes to reporting structure, tooling standardization, or cost discipline in year one-two
- Company size is small enough that an Engineering Manager / Director role may have very limited team size to manage and limited scope for organizational growth
