# Company Research: Flinks

**Company Type:** SaaS / Fintech Platform \
**Stage:** Scale-up (subsidiary of National Bank of Canada) \
**Remote Policy:** Remote-first (Canada)

## Quick Take

- Strong technical product in Open Finance/Open Banking space with solid market position in Canada
- National Bank ownership (80%) provides stability but also brought layoffs and co-founder departures
- French language requirement uncertain - bilingual job postings but remote-first culture may be more flexible than typical Quebec companies

## Company & Product

Flinks is a Canadian embedded finance platform founded in 2016, providing financial data connectivity, intelligence, and payments infrastructure. The platform connects to 15,000+ financial institutions across North America, powers 1M+ monthly connections, and provides 4,500+ real-time financial insights.

**Core products:**
- Data connectivity (consent-driven bank account access via APIs)
- Data enrichment (AI/ML categorization, income verification, risk assessment)
- Payments infrastructure

**Target customers:** Lending, fintech, banking, insurance, wealth management companies

**Locations:**
- HQ: 1001 Rue Lenoir, A114, Montreal, Quebec (Saint-Henri/Mile Ex district)
- Second office: 119 Spadina Ave, Toronto, Ontario

**Size:** ~105-114 employees (down 6% YoY), engineering team estimated 40-60

**Annual Revenue:** ~$35M (estimated)

## Business Stability

**Ownership:** National Bank of Canada (80% since August 2021)

**Funding:** $103M CAD total (including $30M capital injection in 2021)

**Recent news:**
- Co-founders Yves-Gabriel Leboeuf and Frédérick Lavoie left in June 2024 to start competitor "Deck"
- National Bank suing co-founders for $5.7M over non-compete violation
- Employee count down 6% YoY (layoffs mentioned in reviews)
- No major public layoff announcements, but multiple reviews mention "many layoffs"

## Concerns for Engineering Managers

**Red flags identified:**

1. **Layoff history & culture impact** - Multiple Glassdoor reviews mention "many layoffs" and "silent layoffs" affecting team morale and culture

2. **Founder departure drama** - Both technical co-founders left in 2024 to start a competitor, now being sued. This suggests internal tensions post-acquisition

3. **National Bank corporate influence** - Transition from startup to bank subsidiary may have changed culture; reviews mention transparency issues and limited innovation scope

4. **Employee count declining** - 6% reduction suggests ongoing optimization/cuts rather than growth mode

5. **French language uncertainty** - Bilingual job postings and Quebec HQ under National Bank (francophone institution) may require French for people management role, though not explicitly stated

**Mitigating factors:**
- Remote-first policy may reduce French requirement
- Toronto office presence suggests English-acceptable culture
- Strong product-market fit with Open Banking opportunity
- Solid Glassdoor ratings overall

## Language Assessment

**Evidence suggesting French may be required:**
- Montreal HQ in Quebec (subject to Bill 96 language laws)
- Majority owned by National Bank of Canada (historically francophone institution)
- Co-founders had French names (Leboeuf, Lavoie, Cousineau)
- Job posting published in both English and French

**Evidence suggesting English-only may be acceptable:**
- Remote-first work policy
- Toronto office with English-speaking market
- International expansion focus (US market)
- No explicit French requirement in job posting
- Tech industry in Montreal often operates in English

**Recommendation:** Clarify language requirements directly with recruiter before investing significant time in application process
