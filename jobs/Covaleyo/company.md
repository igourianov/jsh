# Covaleyo

- **Company Type:** Vertical market software (lending SaaS for banks and credit unions)
- **Stage:** Enterprise subsidiary (Constellation Software Inc., TSX:CSU)
- **Size:** Small. Headcount not published. ZoomInfo lists 13, which is likely an undercount; the About page alone names 10 managers. Andromeda (parent portfolio) reports 2,500+ employees across 30+ companies.
- **Remote Policy:** Remote in Canada for this role, Eastern time zone preferred. Legacy offices in Markham/Toronto (Valeyo) and Kitchener (Covarity).

## Quick Take
- Two old, sticky Canadian lending products glued together in April 2025: ProLender (loan origination, ~30% of Canadian credit unions) and Covarity (loan monitoring, ~50% of Canada's margined loans). Classic Constellation profile: niche, mission-critical, low growth, cash generative.
- The role sits in a small shop with an "Initiatives" function (Rob Decaire is listed as Initiatives Manager on the About page) and an offshore dev team. Expect a lean budget, hands-on work and Constellation's frugal compensation culture, which matches the below-floor salary band.
- The AI mandate is timely: CSU stock fell over 50% peak to trough by 2026-01 on "SaaSpocalypse" fears, and HQ is pushing subsidiaries to use AI to defend and grow revenue. That gives the role air cover, but also means results will be measured against revenue, not experimentation.

## Milestones
- 2000-2001: Covarity incorporated in Kitchener, ON (founder Jeff Fedor). CRI Canada, the company behind the Criterion LOS (later renamed ProLender), dates to around 2000 per CB Insights; Perseus cites Valeyo as having "40+ years" of client trust, so the lineage is older.
- 2012-04: Covarity acquired by Constellation Software (reported $23M, per Tracxn/PitchBook aggregators).
- 2017-04: Securian Financial Group acquires CRI Canada and Canadian Premier Life. 2017-09 they launch the joint "Valeyo" brand.
- 2023-02: Canadian Premier rebrands to Securian Canada after buying Sun Life's Sponsored Markets business. Valeyo Inc. operates as a sister technology company.
- 2024-04-08: Perseus Group Software Corp. (CSU) buys Valeyo Inc. and its LOS business from Securian Canada. Securian keeps the insurance side. Deal led by Alvin Lau (President, Financial Solutions) and Mitchell Francis (President, Constellation Financial Software).
- 2025-04-14: Covarity and Valeyo merge into Covaleyo. Legal entity stays Perseus Group Software Corp. Nathan Cogger GM, Adam Moore head of Product and Engineering.
- 2025-09: Mark Leonard steps down as CSU President for health reasons; Mark Miller takes over.
- 2026-04: Covaleyo exhibits at the CGI Credit Union Technology Forum (Vancouver) with Digital Lender, ProLender and Covarity.

## Company & Product
Covaleyo sells lending software covering the loan lifecycle for North American banks and credit unions, with retail, commercial and agricultural lending focus.

- **ProLender** (formerly Criterion): loan origination. Digital Lender intake across branch, online and broker channels, "Ideal Lender" decisioning engine, commercial/agri risk rating, provincial regulatory forms, e-signature, core banking integrations (Temenos, Finastra, Fiserv, FIS), Equifax/TransUnion, Sagen and CUMIS mortgage insurance, Lendesk, Flinks, Narrativa, Celero. Named customers include Conexus, FirstOntario, Prosperity, Northern and Lakeland credit unions.
- **Covarity**: commercial loan and covenant monitoring, borrowing base automation, borrower document portal with SSO. Targets community and regional banks ($500M-$50B assets), credit unions and ABL lenders. Claims $39B+ committed capital monitored and 20K+ working capital loans tracked.

**Ownership chain.** Constellation Software Inc. > Perseus Operating Group > Andromeda ("A Perseus Portfolio Group") > Covaleyo. Mitchell Francis, President of Constellation Financial Software within Perseus, is named as Covaleyo's Portfolio Manager, so Constellation Financial Software is effectively the portfolio Covaleyo reports into. Andromeda's lending/mortgage siblings include Optimal Blue, Dark Matter Technologies, Constellation Mortgage Solutions and ReverseVision.

**Perseus HR relationship.** `Perseus_HR@constellationhbs.com` sits on the domain of Constellation HomeBuilder Systems, another Perseus portfolio company. Perseus runs a shared Workday tenant (`talentmanagementsolution.wd3.myworkdayjobs.com/perseus-careers`) for recruiting across its portfolio. HR and recruiting are centralized at Perseus, not run by Covaleyo, so the recruiter is unlikely to know team details and the salary band is set by Perseus policy.

## Engineering Culture
- No engineering blog, no public GitHub, no conference talks found. Engineering is invisible publicly, which is normal for Constellation subsidiaries.
- Structure per the 2025 merger release: one development lead per product (Erena Garcia for ProLender, Dennis Smith for Covarity, now "Development Manager, R&D"), both under Adam Moore (Director, Product and Engineering). The Initiatives role would be a third development manager.
- SOC 2 Type II certified and audited annually, which matches the SOC audit requirement in the posting. A Senior Manager, Information Security (Glen Dsouza) exists on staff.
- Posting mentions a lean offshore team. At least some developers work remotely.
- Constellation's operating model is decentralized: small autonomous business units, tight cost control, best-practice sharing through portfolio peer groups. Expect little central engineering platform and lots of self-reliance.

## Tech Stack
Largely not public.
- Open API with REST and webhooks.
- Covarity is presented as a cloud SaaS platform; ProLender's hosting model is not stated.
- Integrations with SharePoint, Box, DocuSign, Adobe Sign, Power BI and Tableau suggest a Microsoft-leaning ecosystem.
- .NET is likely (developer profiles tied to the company list .NET Framework, MVC/MVVM), but this is weak evidence. Given both products are 20+ years old, expect significant legacy code.
- The posting names no language, only AI-assisted and agentic development tooling.

## Team Health
- **Covaleyo:** no Glassdoor profile yet.
- **Valeyo (Glassdoor):** 3.5/5 from 24 reviews, 56% recommend. Work-life balance 4.2, compensation 3.6, culture 3.3, career opportunities 3.0. Most reviews likely predate the 2024 sale and include the insurance business Securian kept, so they say little about the current software team. Themes: good people and benefits, organizational churn.
- **Constellation Software (Glassdoor):** 4.0/5 from 159 reviews, 82% recommend. Work-life balance 4.1, culture 3.8, career opportunities 3.7, compensation 3.4 (lowest sub-score).
- **Perseus Group (Glassdoor):** 11 reviews. A 2025 manager review cites a good team and fair pay, but undefined career pathing and favoritism at senior levels. A negative review from Perseus's dealership group describes weekend overtime for remote staff, public criticism in meetings, cynicism and infighting. Experience varies heavily by business unit.

## Business Stability
- Very stable ownership. Constellation buys to hold forever and does not flip businesses. No funding risk.
- Strong market share in a regulated, slow-moving niche with high switching costs. Customer base is concentrated in Canadian credit unions, a consolidating sector, which shrinks the customer count over time.
- CSU itself: revenue and EPS still growing (15% and 21% reported), deploying $1.6B on acquisitions in Q1 2026, but the stock more than halved from its 2025 peak on AI disruption fears and the founder's exit. Management's public answer is that subsidiaries will use AI to build new revenue, which is exactly this role.
- Covaleyo is itself a consolidation of two acquired units. Merges like this in Constellation usually come with cost rationalization; no layoffs were found in news, but none would be publicized at this size.

## Red Flags
- **Compensation:** Constellation's lowest Glassdoor sub-score is pay, and the posted band (CAD $135-165K, no bonus mentioned) confirms it.
- **Tiny org, thin ladder:** a unit of this size has no room to grow into Director. Perseus reviews call career pathing undefined.
- **Legacy code and offshore team:** two 20+ year old products and a lean offshore team suggest the "AI innovation" charter may collide with maintenance and integration work.
- **Centralized HR via a sibling company's mailbox:** recruiting is run by Perseus, so expect slower, more generic process and limited insight into the team until the hiring manager call.
- **Culture varies by unit:** Perseus has credible negative reviews from other business units. Covaleyo-specific culture is unknown; ask the hiring manager directly.
- **Parent under AI pressure:** the mandate is real, but so is the scrutiny. Expect ROI questions early.
