# Clubspeed

- **Company Type:** SaaS / Platform (venue management software)
- **Stage:** Acquired portfolio company (PE-backed, part of Valsoft Corporation)
- **Size:** ~38 employees (across North America and Europe, as of mid-2025)
- **Remote Policy:** Not clearly published; job postings suggest a mix of in-office (Irvine/Corona, CA HQ) and remote roles

## Quick Take
- Small team (~38 people) for a platform serving 1,700+ venues in 34 countries - lean engineering org with broad surface area
- Just changed hands again (June 2026, acquired by Valsoft's Manos Software Group) - second ownership change since 2019, typical PE "buy and hold" vertical software model
- Glassdoor rating is solid (4.0/5) but one review flags a "mass exodus of 30-40 people" tied to management punitive style and perk cuts - worth probing in interviews

## Milestones
- Founded 2005, headquartered in Corona/Irvine, CA
- April 2017: Nadavon Capital Partners makes initial investment
- March 2019: Nadavon Capital Partners acquires majority stake
- November 2021: Acquires Vantora (online booking/waiver software for axe-throwing, paintball, and similar venues) - Vantora continues operating as its own product
- Also acquired KartingManager and Resova along the way, expanding into adjacent entertainment-venue software niches
- June 2, 2026: Acquired by Valsoft Corporation through its Manos Software Group subsidiary; company said to continue operating independently with existing team in place

## Company & Product
Clubspeed provides venue management software for go-kart tracks, trampoline parks, family entertainment centers, escape rooms, and other attractions. The platform covers point-of-sale, scheduling/booking, waivers, customer management, marketing, and analytics, with an iPad app for guest-facing use and a host/operator dashboard (Club-Director). Serves 1,700+ locations across 34 countries. Product line has grown through bolt-on acquisitions (Vantora, KartingManager, Resova) rather than pure organic build-out, so the org likely carries multiple codebases/stacks under one roof.

## Engineering Culture
Public engineering-blog presence is minimal - no visible dev blog or open-source activity found. Recent job postings reference CI/CD, security scanning, deploy hygiene, and on-call/incident-response practices, suggesting the team is actively modernizing engineering process rather than having mature DevOps already in place. No independent evidence of engineering-specific culture surveys; general company Glassdoor reviews describe transparent, informal upper-management communication style.

## Tech Stack
- Frameworks: Angular, .NET, Laravel (multi-stack, likely a result of acquiring separate products like Vantora)
- Cloud: AWS (ECS, CloudFront)
- Frontend/web tooling: jQuery, HTML5, various marketing/analytics tags (HubSpot, Google Analytics, Piwik)
- Overall picture is a fairly conventional, somewhat dated-leaning enterprise SaaS stack rather than a cutting-edge one; multi-framework spread across products acquired independently

## Team Health
- Glassdoor: 4.0/5 overall (57 reviews), 75% would recommend to a friend, 65% positive business outlook
- Sub-ratings: work-life balance 3.7/5, culture & values 3.9/5, career opportunities 3.8/5, comp & benefits 3.6/5
- Positive reviews cite trust between teammates/managers, transparent and fun upper-management communication
- Negative reviews cite management that is "quick to blame and punish," perk rollbacks, and a period with a "mass exodus of 30-40 people within a year" - unclear if this predates or postdates the Nadavon/Valsoft ownership changes
- Interview process rated positively (100% positive experience) with low-to-moderate difficulty (2/5)
- Great Place to Work certified, with 90% of employees saying it's a great place to work vs. 57% typical US company (self-reported/certification-body figure, treat with some skepticism given the mixed Glassdoor commentary)

## Business Stability
- Backed by Nadavon Capital Partners (2017-2026), now owned by Valsoft Corporation via Manos Software Group (since June 2026) - Valsoft is an established acquirer of vertical-market software businesses with a buy-and-hold, "operate independently" model
- No layoffs reported in recent news; financial terms of the Valsoft deal were not disclosed
- Growth has come substantially through acquisition (Vantora, KartingManager, Resova) rather than pure organic expansion, consistent with typical PE vertical-SaaS roll-up strategy

## Red Flags
- Ownership just changed hands again (second time in ~7 years) - expect potential reorganization, process changes, or leadership churn in the near term as Valsoft integrates
- Glassdoor review referencing a "mass exodus of 30-40 people" tied to punitive management style is a specific and concrete concern worth asking about directly in interviews
- Multiple acquired products (Vantora, KartingManager, Resova) on different stacks (Angular/.NET/Laravel) likely means fragmented codebases and integration debt
- Very small engineering org (~38 employees company-wide) means limited internal mobility/backup and higher single-point-of-failure risk
