## Summary

A horizontal, non-managerial engineering-effectiveness function sitting across Euna's Development and IS organizations. The job is to instrument delivery, measure it against DORA/SPACE benchmarks, find the waste, and then translate the findings into ROI models that Finance, the VP of Software Development and the CTO will act on. Authority comes entirely from the quality of the analysis: the posting explicitly rules out oversight and frames the seat as advisory.

Half the role is engineering process work and half is financial analysis. The financial half is not decorative. Building investment cases that "resonate with Finance", modelling ROI on productivity spend, and presenting those narratives to the C-suite are stated as core, not nice-to-have. The posting is asking for someone who can hold their own in a budget conversation about developer tooling.

The role fills an existing vacancy, so the function already exists and someone left it. Euna uses AI in candidate screening and says so up front.

## Company

Euna Solutions builds purpose-built cloud software for public sector and government organizations: procurement, budgeting, payments, grants management and special education administration. The suite is a full-cycle financial platform for government buyers, with AI features and intelligent automation layered on for compliance and decision support.

More than 3,600 organizations across North America use it. Recognized on Government Technology's GovTech 100 list. 501-1,000 employees, Canadian presence in Oakville, ON.

The company positions itself around trust, transparency and compliance for public sector customers, which is a slower, procurement-driven sales motion than commercial B2B SaaS. Internally it runs a Development organization and a separate IS organization, both of which this role spans, with a VP of Software Development and a CTO above it.

Culture messaging leans on an explicit "human + AI" stance: employees are encouraged to use AI tooling, and the company applies AI to candidate screening. Perks are modest and conventional: health and dental, two extra wellness days a year, culture and community committees, and an explicit "honest 40-hour week" commitment.
